\section{Basic examples}

\ex<ex:basic>
\begingl
\gla Ich habe den Mann gesehen//
\glb I have.\textsc{1sg} the.\textsc{acc} man see.\textsc{ptcp}//
\glft `I have seen the man.'//
\endgl
\xe

As shown in (\getref{ex:basic}), German is fine. Now an ungrammatical one, see (\nextx):

\ex
\ljudge{*}\begingl
\gla Ich Mann habe gesehen den//
\glb I man have seen the//
\glft `Intended: I have seen the man.'//
\endgl
\xe

The previous example was (\lastx) and (\lastx{}) is bad. Next, sub-examples:

\pex<ex:parts>
Consider the following data from Turkish and Basque:
\a<tur> \begingl
\gla Ali kitab{\i}-n{\i} oku-du//
\glb Ali book-\textsc{3sg.poss-acc} read-\textsc{pst}//
\glft `Ali read his book.'//
\endgl
\a<eus> \begingl
\gla Jon-ek liburua irakurri du//
\glb Jon-\textsc{erg} book.\textsc{abs} read \textsc{aux}//
\glft `Jone has read the book.'//
\endgl
\a A plain text part with \textit{italics} and \textbf{bold}, plus \'{e}, \"o, and \c{c}.
\xe

Compare (\getfullref{ex:parts.tur}) with (\getfullref{ex:parts.eus}); (\getref{ex:parts.eus}) is Basque.
